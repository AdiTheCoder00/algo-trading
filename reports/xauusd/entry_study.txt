====================================================================================================
VERDICT
====================================================================================================

  Nothing survived all three tests.
      No filter beat both the unfiltered baseline and every random control in both out-of-sample
      windows while leaving enough trades to matter. Read that as the result it is: the entry's
      problem is not that it is insufficiently selective. Being more selective removes trades
      roughly at random, which is exactly what the control rows do on purpose.

  What the in-sample column would have told you, and why not to listen.
      The best filter in sample is '1. ATR >= 8x spread' at +4.35 a trade against the baseline's
      -0.10. Out of sample the same filter makes -8.08 against a baseline of +0.11. That gap is the
      entire argument for why the in-sample table is in this report only as a warning.

  How much a filter can 'earn' by doing nothing.
      Throwing away 25%, 50% and 75% of signals at random moves expectancy across out of sample
      +0.68 to +2.00; holdout +3.29 to +4.56. Any real filter has to clear that band before its
      number means anything, and several of the filters above do not.

  The finding this does not change.
      None of these rows makes the strategy profitable out of sample after costs. Filtering
      redistributes a zero-expectancy process; it does not create an edge in one. If the entry is to
      be fixed, it needs a different signal rather than a stricter reading of this one - and that is
      a new strategy, not a variant of this one, and should be measured as such from scratch.
