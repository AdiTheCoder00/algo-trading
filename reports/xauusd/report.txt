================================================================================================
5. FINAL EVALUATION  (the brief's fifteen questions, from the numbers above)
================================================================================================

  1. Is it profitable after realistic transaction costs?
      No. Net -65.56 over 476 trades: profit factor 0.961, expectancy -0.14 a trade. Before
      costs the same trades made +198.29 and costs took 263.85. The strategy is not losing
      to the market; it is losing to the spread.

  2. Is it profitable out of sample?
      No. In sample -34.03 on 331 trades, out of sample -46.38 on 145; the change is -12.34.
      On the second holdout - 244 trades on the broker's own bars over eight months the
      archive does not cover - it made +872.67, profit factor 1.420. Every one of these
      samples is small enough that a different split would move the number.

  3. Is the edge present on both BUY and SELL?
      No. BUY -83.34 over 302 trades; SELL +17.78 over 174. They point opposite ways, and
      with gold trending up through most of this window that is at least as likely to be the
      trend as an edge in one direction.

  4. What percentage of trades hit the stop?
      22.3% - 106 trades, at a stop of 6 x ATR. Under the specified flat $10 it is a
      different number in every regime, which is why the baseline no longer uses one - see
      the stop table.

  5. What percentage exit through the RSI reversal?
      26.9% - 128 trades. Note what that means: in three quarters of trades RSI never
      reached its extreme at all, so the exit the rules describe most carefully is the one
      that fires least.

  6. What percentage reach the 4-hour maximum hold?
      50.8% - 242 trades, the most common ending by a wide margin. The clock, not either
      signal, is what actually closes this strategy's positions.

  7. Does the RSI reversal exit improve results?
      No. Switching it off is -75.83 on net P&L. It raises the win rate (48.9% with it,
      42.4% without) and loses money doing so, which is the signature of an exit that closes
      winners early.

  8. Does the news filter improve results?
      No, and it barely acts. Switching it off is +30.89 on net P&L across 5 blocked
      entries. A difference built from 5 trades is not a measurement of the filter - and the
      calendar covers only NFP, CPI, PPI and FOMC, so a wider one would block more.

  9. How often does the -$50 daily loss limit activate?
      Never in this window: 1 days, 1 entries blocked. One position of 0.01 lots with a stop
      of a few dollars would need five losing trades inside one 21:00-21:00 trading day, and
      four-hour holds leave no room for that. The rule is inert at this size.

  10. What is the maximum drawdown?
      229.65 on realised equity - 22.2% of a 1,000.00 account. Against a net result of
      roughly zero, that is the whole point: the path is far larger than the destination.

  11. What is the worst losing streak?
      9 losing trades in a row (the best winning streak is 9).

  12. What is the largest single-trade loss?
      -33.71, against a stop that ranged from 9.79 at the median to 43.48 at its widest.
      That is the ATR stop working as intended rather than failing: a trade entered in a
      volatile hour risks more dollars, and it is the fixed-dollar version that was quietly
      taking a different amount of risk each time without saying so. 3 bars also opened past
      the level and filled at the open, and every exit still crosses the spread.

  13. How sensitive is it to transaction costs?
      Completely - costs are the result. realistic -65.56, moderate -270.31, conservative
      -649.85. At 0.55 a trade against an average win of +6.97, the cost and the edge are
      the same size, so the venue decides the sign.

  14. Is performance reasonably stable across years?
      There is only one full year here plus a stub, so the honest unit is the fold: 3 of 6
      are positive, spanning -36.18 to +61.39 on 55-76 trades each. That spread is what a
      zero-expectancy process looks like cut six ways. The sharper answer comes from the
      holdout: gold's median five-minute range is more than three times larger there than in
      the study window. That is exactly what the volatility-scaled stop exists to absorb,
      and it is why the baseline no longer uses a fixed dollar distance. The win rate still
      falls from 48.9% to 54.1% for that reason alone. A rule set whose risk changes with
      the price of gold is not stable across regimes even when its P&L happens to be.

  15. Is there evidence of overfitting?
      Not in this run, because nothing was fitted: no parameter was searched, the baseline
      was run first and reported before any variant, and the variants sit beside it rather
      than replacing it. What the sweeps do show is fragility - the sign of the P&L flips on
      the holding period, on the stop size, on direction and on the reversal exit. A rule
      set with a real edge does not change sign every time you nudge it.

  WHAT THIS SAMPLE CANNOT TELL YOU
      476 trades over sixteen months is a small sample for a difference this small. An
      expectancy of -0.14 against an average win of +6.97 is well inside the noise a few
      hundred coin flips would produce, so the reading is not 'this loses slightly' but
      'this has no edge that this data can measure, and it pays the spread to find out'. The
      result is also from a Dukascopy tick archive, not from the broker this would trade on;
      a different spread would move the number, though not far enough to change the
      conclusion.
