EXECUTION AND COST ASSUMPTIONS
  entry             at the OPEN of the candle after the confirmation candle
  sizing            risk / distance-to-stop, ROUNDED DOWN to whole ounces;
                    a setup needing less than one ounce is skipped, never
                    rounded up into a larger risk than intended
  stop              the pullback extreme, checked intrabar; a candle that gaps
                    through it fills at that candle's open, never at the level
  4-hour cap        measured from the actual fill timestamp, closed at the first
                    executable price at or after it
  same-candle       the stop is tested before any close-based exit, so a candle
                    containing both resolves as the stop - the engine's existing
                    pessimistic convention
  exits             stop and 4-hour cap ONLY. No take-profit, no trail, no EMA
                    exit, no news filter, no daily loss limit.

8. FINAL ASSESSMENT
====================================================================================================

  1. Does the baseline have positive expectancy?
      No. -2.11 a trade over 2566 trades, net -5,425.57 on a 10,000.00 nominal account. The win rate
      is 18.7% against an average win of +28.49 and an average loss of -9.16 - the winners are three
      times the size of the losers, and there are nowhere near enough of them.

  2. Is profit factor meaningfully above 1?
      No - it is 0.716, meaningfully BELOW 1. For every dollar the winners made, the losers lost
      about a dollar and forty. This is not a marginal result that better execution might rescue.

  3. Is drawdown acceptable relative to returns?
      No. Max drawdown 5,780.07 against a net of -5,425.57 - the drawdown IS the result. The worst
      losing streak is 30 trades against a best winning streak of 5, which is the shape of a
      strategy that is right occasionally and large when it is.

  4. Is performance consistent across time?
      Consistently negative: 2 of 17 months are positive. That is a kind of consistency, and not the
      useful kind - there is no regime in this window where the strategy worked and the rest where
      it did not.

  5. Does it survive realistic transaction costs?
      No, and costs are the whole story. Gross P&L before costs is +1,493.89; costs took 6,919.46,
      which is 2.70 a trade. realistic -5,425.57, moderate -11,030.18, conservative -19,935.20.
      Risk-based sizing is what makes this so severe: a tight structural stop buys a large position,
      and the spread is charged on every ounce of it.

  6. Does it survive out of sample?
      In sample -4,181.95, out of sample -1,267.36 - negative in both halves. The MT5 holdout is
      +873.55 at profit factor 1.109, but read it with two things in mind: 632 setups there were
      skipped as unaffordable against 54 here, because that market's ranges are three times wider,
      and the signal study already showed that a four-hour position taken at random made money in
      that window.

  7. Is performance robust around 9/20/50/200?
      The parameter surface is flat and uniformly negative - 12 of 12 single-EMA variants lose
      money, and the spread between the best and worst is small next to the drawdown of any of them.
      So yes, it is robust, in the sense that nothing about the answer depends on the exact periods.
      There is no cliff here because there is no peak to fall off.

  8. Is there evidence of overfitting?
      No, and there could not be - nothing was fitted. The baseline ran and was reported before any
      variant existed and no parameter was searched. The flat sensitivity surface in section 3 is
      the positive evidence for that: an overfitted parameter set sits on a spike, and this one sits
      on a plain.

  THE LIMITATION THAT SHAPES THIS RESULT
      54 setups were skipped because their pullback stop sat more than $10 from entry, and at a
      one-ounce minimum a $10 budget cannot take those. More importantly, the median stop that WAS
      taken is only 2.09 of price, which buys a median 4-ounce position. That is the mechanism:
      sizing to a fixed dollar risk against a tight structural stop makes the position large, and
      the spread is charged per ounce. The strategy does not lose because the pullback idea is wrong
      - gross P&L is positive - it loses because this way of sizing it multiplies the cost of being
      right.

  LONG VS SHORT
      LONG -2,961.53 over 1585 trades at 19.8%; SHORT -2,464.04 over 981 at 16.9%. Both lose, and
      neither is rescued by dropping the other. Reported rather than removed, as the specification
      asks.

  THE EXIT TEST CHANGES THE ANSWER, AND NOT IN THE WAY IT LOOKS
      Taking the stop off and trading a flat ounce turns -5,425.57 into +36.61 over 1431 trades, at
      a win rate of 47.3% rather than 18.7%. That is the same finding the RSI strategy's stop study
      produced, arrived at from a different direction: a stop tight enough to fire constantly, sized
      so that tightness buys a larger position, converts noise into realised loss and pays the
      spread on every ounce of it. But read what it actually is - +0.03 a trade at a profit factor
      of 1.007. Removing the stop removes the loss; it does not produce a gain. And it buys that
      with an unbounded single trade: the worst one here is -67.19 on one ounce, 6.7 times the risk
      budget the stop existed to enforce, because the four-hour cap bounds time and not size.

  THE EMA EXIT MAKES IT WORSE, CONSISTENTLY
      Every EMA exit loses more than no exit at all: 4h only +36.61, close < EMA9 -2,156.14, close <
      EMA20 -1,185.12, EMA9/20 cross -663.17. They are ordered by how often they fire - the faster
      the exit, the worse the result - which is what it looks like when the exit is not selecting
      anything and each firing is simply another round trip's spread. On this strategy the honest
      exit is the clock.

  WHAT WOULD ACTUALLY TEST THE IDEA
      The exit test above already did the first of them - a flat size, which is what removed the
      loss. What is left is the same two that ended the RSI study: a venue where the round trip is a
      fraction of this one's, and a longer horizon, where the move available grows faster than the
      cost of taking it. The break-even the no-stop row reaches is not an edge, but it is the first
      arrangement of this strategy where costs are not larger than everything it earns - which is
      the precondition for there being anything to find.
